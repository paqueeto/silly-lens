# YouTube checklist

YouTube's policies change. Before publishing a channel, check the current pages in
YouTube Help for: "Made for kids", "Disclosing altered or synthetic content", and
"YouTube channel monetization policies" (the inauthentic content rule). The notes
below are a starting point, not legal advice.

## Every upload

- **Audience: "Yes, it's made for kids."** Required by law (COPPA) for content aimed
  at children; YouTube and the FTC enforce it. Made-for-kids videos lose comments,
  personalised ads, the mini-player and notifications. That is expected; don't
  mislabel to get them back.
- **Altered or synthetic content.** The disclosure is aimed at realistic content
  (real people saying things they didn't, realistic fake events or places). A clearly
  animated felt cartoon is generally not in scope, and an AI narrator that isn't
  imitating a real person usually isn't either. If unsure, disclose: it costs
  nothing. Never clone a real person's voice without written permission.
- **Chapters** (compilations): the first at 0:00, at least 3, each at least 10
  seconds. `npm run hour` writes them to `out/<name>.youtube.txt`.
- **Title**: the character plus the feeling or event, e.g. "Olive the Owl Learns to
  Fly at Night | Calm Bedtime Story for Kids". Thumbnail: one character, big eyes,
  one bright prop, 2–3 words at most.

## The rule that demonetises copycat channels

YouTube Partner Program content must be original and authentic. Mass-produced,
repetitive or template-swapped videos are not monetisable (this was called
"repetitious content", and was renamed and clarified as "inauthentic content" in
2025). To stay on the right side:

- Every episode is a genuinely new story: a new problem, a new place, new lines.
  Not the same script with the animal swapped.
- Keep a consistent world and cast (a series bible), and keep adding new scenes and
  kit pieces. Don't loop the same scenes across episodes.
- Compilations are fine when they compile your own distinct episodes.
- Avoid an upload schedule that only works by cutting corners on the above.

## Before you scale a channel

- Upload a few episodes and watch retention in YouTube Studio. Kids' content lives
  on rewatches, so shorter, calmer and more repetitive language often wins.
- Keep the project files: every frame here is reproducible from code, which is also
  your proof of original work.
